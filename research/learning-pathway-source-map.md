# Learning pathway source map

**Research date:** 3 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Identify high-trust, original source frameworks that can inform a broad, field-neutral catalog of learning paths. These are topic-selection references, not a claim that this platform awards a degree, accreditation, license, or credit transfer.

## Synthesis

The university and professional frameworks reviewed converge on a broad core: organizations and strategy; economics and quantitative reasoning; accounting and finance; people and leadership; marketing; operations and supply chains; information and technology systems; data and analytics; communication, ethics, and applied decision-making. They also treat specialist areas as distinct choices rather than academic-year stages. The catalog can therefore offer many independent paths and let learners choose a field, with a suggested visual flow and no prerequisite gates between checkpoints.

## Study-plan subject cross-check

The supplied [study plan](../assets/sources/BIS_study_plan.pdf) was used only as a subject-name coverage checklist. Its course descriptions, course codes, class-year divisions, and degree structure are not reproduced in the platform. The subject names were mapped to broad routes covering economics; financial, cost, and management accounting; business management and law; business mathematics and quantitative methods; organizational behavior and communication; operations and marketing; business analytics; logistics and supply chains; finance and investment; management information systems; programming and databases; artificial intelligence and business; entrepreneurship and strategy; systems analysis; IT infrastructure, cloud and connected devices; technology risk; accounting information systems; enterprise systems; data mining and analytics; feasibility; digital business; human resources; applied statistics; information-systems strategy; public finance; and applied projects.

The current library expands that coverage into 517 independent pathways across 14 catalog families, with 46,104 mapped checkpoints. Routes range from 88 to 160 topics. Each checkpoint has four localized, searchable subtopic links, and each route links to five neighboring pathways. The map may suggest a learning flow, while every checkpoint remains open and its completion is tracked independently. Arabic labels are independently written in Arabic, with no English names left in the Arabic interface. Future changes should keep course-year divisions out of the product and preserve learners' choice to study in any order.

## Supplied professional-roadmap cross-check

Six user-supplied roadmap PDFs were used as subject-coverage references, not as instructions or text to reproduce: `engineering-manager.pdf`, `product-manager.pdf`, `python-data-analysis.pdf`, `data-analyst.pdf`, `bi-analyst.pdf`, and `postgresql-dba.pdf`. Their subject maps informed independent role and specialist routes, while the learning checkpoints and bilingual explanations here are original.

- Engineering management: technical direction, architecture and quality, delivery and operations, incidents and reliability, hiring, coaching, team development, planning, risk, and stakeholder communication.
- Product management: discovery, research, strategy, roadmaps, requirements, user experience, delivery, launch, metrics, experimentation, growth, and portfolio decisions.
- Python data analysis, data analysis, and business intelligence: programming and data structures, SQL, collection and cleaning, statistics, exploratory analysis, visualization, reporting, data models and platforms, governance, and applied projects.
- Database administration: relational foundations, setup and configuration, security, backup and recovery, high availability and replication, monitoring, performance, maintenance, and automation.
- Neighboring routes connect these specialties to accounting, finance, economics, operations, systems, leadership, communication, psychology, and other applied fields. A shared link offers another optional path; it does not act as a prerequisite gate.

## Communication, psychology, and behavioral topics

The expanded catalog adds standalone pathways for written and oral communication, listening, collaboration, leadership, conflict resolution, negotiation, presentation, stakeholder communication, cross-cultural communication, and nonverbal communication. Psychology routes cover scientific foundations and selected subfields, including cognitive, social, developmental, health, educational, consumer, and industrial-organizational psychology. These are learning guides, not clinical advice or professional credentials.

The [American Psychological Association's overview of psychology subfields](https://www.apa.org/education-career/guide/subfields) supports the breadth of this taxonomy, while its [industrial-organizational psychology specialty page](https://www.apa.org/ed/graduate/specialize/industrial) describes evidence-based study of behavior in workplace settings. For communication and collaboration skills, the [Project Management Institute's Power Skills material](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/learning/thought-leadership/shaping-the-future-of-project-management-with-ai.pdf) identifies communication, problem-solving, collaborative leadership, and strategic thinking as relevant interpersonal capabilities.

The body-language route focuses on observing behavior in context, culture, and interaction, and on the limits of interpreting intent. It does not teach a checklist for detecting lies. A meta-analysis of 1,338 estimates across 158 potential deception cues found that many cues showed no discernible or only weak links to deception ([DePaulo et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.129.1.74)).

## Primary source frameworks and what they support

- [Harvard Business School MBA curriculum](https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/curriculum) — Its published required curriculum spans finance, financial reporting, leadership and organizational behavior, marketing, strategy, technology and operations, business and the international economy, data science and AI, entrepreneurship, and accountability. Useful as a broad business-core cross-check, not a template to reproduce.
- [Wharton undergraduate concentrations](https://undergrad.wharton.upenn.edu/concentrations/) and [official undergraduate course descriptions](https://oid.wharton.upenn.edu/programs/undergraduate/course-descriptions/) — Confirm distinct areas including accounting, analytics, finance, management, marketing, operations, information and decisions, entrepreneurship, risk, statistics, healthcare, real estate, and business economics. Their operations descriptions also surface forecasting, capacity, scheduling, inventory, service operations, optimization, and supply-chain coordination.
- [AACSB Global Standards for Business Education](https://www.aacsb.edu/educators/global-standards) — The 2026 standard-setting framework emphasizes business curricula that integrate theory and practice, evolve with business and technology, and develop analytical, digital, and ethical capabilities. It describes quality-improvement expectations; it does not prescribe this platform's topics.
- [AACSB 2026 Accounting Accreditation Standards](https://www.aacsb.edu/-/media/documents/accreditation/2026/2026-accounting-standards.pdf) — Supports accounting routes covering curriculum outcomes, learning assessment, digital agility, professional practice, and ongoing curriculum improvement.
- [CFA Institute Program Curriculum](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/curriculum) — Current professional investment topics include quantitative methods, economics, financial statement analysis, corporate finance, equity, fixed income, derivatives and risk, alternatives, portfolio construction, ethics, and professional standards.
- [ACM/AIS IS2020 model curriculum](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/education/curricula-recommendations/is2020.pdf) — An internationally oriented undergraduate information-systems competency model. It describes common IS competencies and elective areas, with adaptability across computing, business, and information-management settings.
- [ACM Data Science Task Force report](https://dstf.acm.org/DSTF_Final_Report.pdf) — Frames data science as an interdisciplinary lifecycle: computational and statistical foundations; data acquisition, curation, management, and processing; analysis and modeling; interpretation and communication; and professional responsibility. It provides a cross-check for data-analysis, data-science, and data-engineering routes.
- [INFORMS undergraduate business analytics curriculum article](https://doi.org/10.1287/ited.2014.0134) — The ACM report summarizes its curriculum building blocks as data management, descriptive analytics, visualization, predictive and prescriptive analytics, data mining, and an analytics practicum. This is useful for organizing a business-focused analytics branch.
- [ACM IT2017 curriculum guidelines](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/education/curricula-recommendations/it2017.pdf) and [ACM CS2023 curriculum report](https://csed.acm.org/) — Support separate pathways for IT infrastructure and service capabilities, core computing, algorithms, software systems, security, data, and professional dispositions.
- [CSEC2017 cybersecurity curricular guidelines](https://cybered.hosting.acm.org/wp/) and [NIST NICE Framework](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/181/r1/final) — Support cybersecurity as a broad discipline spanning technology, people, information, processes, organizational context, risk, and job roles rather than only a list of tools.
- [ASCM SCOR Digital Standard](https://scor.ascm.org/) — Defines a supply-chain map around Orchestrate, Plan, Order, Source, Transform, Fulfill, and Return, with associated metrics, practices, and skills. A supply-chain path can use this process language while retaining original explanations and maps.
- [IIBA Business Analysis Standard task map](https://www.iiba.org/knowledgehub/business-analysis-standard/4-tasks-and-knowledge-areas/introducing-business-analysis-tasks/) — Groups business-analysis tasks into six knowledge areas and notes that real initiatives may be iterative rather than strictly linear. This supports connecting related concepts while keeping each checkpoint independently accessible.
- [PMI PMBOK Guide](https://www.pmi.org/pt-br/standards/pmbok) — The current eighth edition retains project principles and performance-domain foundations while adding clearer process guidance and wider coverage such as AI and procurement. Use the current edition as a topic cross-check; avoid copying proprietary guide text.
- [American Marketing Association Marketing Competency Model](https://www.ama.org/ama-marketing-competency-model/) — Its 2025 model covers ethics, essential capabilities, strategy and planning, content and branding, data and analytics, and channels and technology.
- [SHRM 2026 Body of Applied Skills and Knowledge](https://www.shrm.org/content/dam/en/shrm/credentials/shrm-certification/bask/shrm-bask.pdf) — Provides HR knowledge domains across people, organization, and workplace, supported by behavioral competencies. Useful for separating HR specializations such as talent, workforce, learning, rewards, relations, organization development, and workplace risk.
- [APA psychology subfields](https://www.apa.org/education-career/guide/subfields) and [recognized professional psychology specialties](https://www.apa.org/ed/graduate/specialize/recognized) — Support distinguishing research subfields and professional specialties; their scope should not be collapsed into one generic psychology path.
- [PMI Power Skills material](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/learning/thought-leadership/shaping-the-future-of-project-management-with-ai.pdf) — Supports practical paths in communication, collaborative leadership, problem-solving, and strategic thinking.
- [PostgreSQL server administration documentation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/admin.html) — Primary technical reference for database administration topics such as maintenance, backup and restore, availability, monitoring, and reliability.

## Use in this product

Use source headings to choose or verify topic names, then write original bilingual descriptions and examples. Link each selected topic to topic-specific public reading, video, or scholarly searches in the active interface language. Keep any source logo, diagram, proprietary explanation, or course content out of the map unless separately licensed. Show a framework name only when it helps a learner find a source; do not brand the product or a roadmap as a university curriculum.

These sources were selected for curriculum or professional-framework authority. Their scope, level, and jurisdiction differ, so no one framework should be presented as globally recognized accreditation for every route.
